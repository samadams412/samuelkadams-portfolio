## Problem

CS3113 Principles of Cybersecurity assigned a six-person team, operating under the name Death Star Consulting, to assess the cybersecurity posture of NARO Inc., a fictitious nonprofit doing R&D on mini wind turbines. The scope covered physical access controls across NARO's two office buildings, network and remote-access security across its 17 servers, and the maturity of its cybersecurity policies. It was a documentation review: no active penetration testing or direct system interaction, a limitation the report states explicitly rather than leaving implied.

## Approach

NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 structured the assessment across its six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover. The team adapted that framework with Sandia National Laboratories' IDART methodology, a process-oriented approach that ties each finding to evidence from NARO's own documented processes rather than general best-practice assumptions. Findings were sorted into Weaknesses, Strengths, and Observations, each carrying a severity rating of High, Moderate, or Low and mapped to a specific CSF 2.0 function and subcategory.

Two findings from the Weaknesses section, both rated High, show how that mapping worked. NARO relied on a single factor, username and password, for all remote VPN access, which the report flagged as a single point of failure and mapped to CSF 2.0 Protect, PR.AA-03. Separately, NARO's Supermicro R&D servers ran Ubuntu 18.04.6 LTS, which stopped receiving security patches in May 2023, mapped to CSF 2.0 Protect, PR.PS-02. These are two findings out of a full set covering physical access, network segmentation, and policy gaps, not the complete report.

## My role

I built every figure in the report, including the network topology diagrams and the data flow diagram above. I also wrote Section 6, Assessment Results and Recommendations, the full Weaknesses/Strengths/Observations writeup, and mapped each weakness and observation to its NIST CSF 2.0 function and subcategory.

## Outcome

The report was delivered as an independent evaluation NARO's (fictitious) leadership could use to prioritize cost-effective mitigations without assuming dedicated security staff or a large security budget. It runs about 30 pages; this write-up summarizes it rather than reproducing it.
